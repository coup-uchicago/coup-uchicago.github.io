Kuvia

Kuvia is UChicago’s (in)famous weeklong Winter Quarter tradition! It was founded by former Dean of the College Donald Levine in 1983 to reframe winter as an exciting time. Kuvia consisted of Kuviasungnerk and Kangeiko. Kuviasungnerk, from which Kuvia takes its name, means “happy times” in the Inuit language and was a variety of afternoon activities. This is also where one of Kuvia’s old nicknames, “Nerk Week," comes from. Kangeiko, which means “calisthenics” in Japanese, is Kuvia’s early morning exercises.

The first four days of Kuvia take place at the Henry Crown Fieldhouse and consist of Sun Salutations (a yoga exercise), workshops by various RSOs, and bagels and juice to end the morning. On the fifth and final day of Kuvia, participants follow the polar bear to Promontory Point and do Sun Salutations on the coast of Lake Michigan. Participants who attend all five days of Kuvia win a coveted long-sleeved t-shirt. The t-shirt design is often a winning submission from a student t-shirt design contest held in Autumn Quarter.
